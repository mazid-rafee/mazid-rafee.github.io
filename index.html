About

Research that connects modern machine learning with the structure of scientific problems.

I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science at Florida International University, conducting research in the High Performance Database Research Center under Prof. Naphtali Rishe. My research contributes to NSF Award #2018611, supporting AI-driven analysis of multimodal environmental observations. My work asks how learning systems can use heterogeneous observations, physical knowledge, and interpretable representations when scientific data are incomplete, noisy, or imperfectly aligned.

My current dissertation research studies SAR-guided conditional diffusion bridges for reconstructing cloud-obscured multispectral satellite imagery. My broader work includes multispectral cloud segmentation, physics-guided atmospheric-correction emulation, irregular environmental forecasting, and prototype-based interpretable computer vision.

Dissertation ResearchOngoing

MSCloudr

Multimodal Cloud Removal with Optical + SAR Imagery

Conditional diffusion-bridge methods for reconstructing cloud-obscured 13-band Sentinel-2 imagery using Sentinel-1 SAR as complementary structural information.

  • Mean-reverting bridge dynamics
  • Reliability-aware SAR conditioning
  • Cross-modal alignment and bridge consistency
  • Risk-aware trajectory design
Remote Sensing 2026

pKANrtm

Physics-Guided Atmospheric-Correction Surrogate

A multi-fidelity Kolmogorov-Arnold Network that uses low-cost 6S simulations as physical guidance and learns corrections toward high-fidelity libRadtran outputs.

Published in Remote Sensing · FIU Invention Disclosure D2026-0057

IEEE CAI 2026

MSCloudCAM

Multispectral Cloud Segmentation

A multi-sensor model combining a Vision Transformer backbone, multi-scale contextual modeling, and cross-attention for four-class cloud segmentation across Sentinel-2 and Landsat-8 imagery.

Interpretable AI

Prototype-Based Video Saliency

Intervention-Oriented Interpretability

Prototype-grounded visual explanations that connect learned concepts to spatial saliency predictions and test whether suppressing an explanatory prototype produces a meaningful change in model behavior.
